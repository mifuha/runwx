A useful pattern. Still an incomplete explanation.

  • Different runners enter each year. Age, training and field strength can change.
  • “Fastest” describes results in this dataset, not verified elite status. The rest are not necessarily amateurs. These groups are chosen using finishing times.
  • Every group shares the same six-hour weather estimate. It is not each runner's actual exposure.

We can describe the association. We cannot say temperature caused the pace differences.

One further check / participant mix

Does who enters explain the pattern?

Different people enter each year. We gave every edition the same mix of recorded age and gender groups, then checked the pattern again.

Warmer editions still tended to have slower median pace. The median correlation barely changed.

Both comparisons use the same 541,659 captured results. There are 16 edition-level weather observations. The four age bands are under 40, 40–49, 50–59 and 60+, within each recorded gender.

This adjustment holds broad recorded categories constant. Training, ability, variation within age bands and incomplete captures remain unresolved. It does not establish a causal heat effect.

How we checked the participant mix

We averaged the group proportions across the 16 editions, giving each edition equal influence. That fixed mix was applied to the full duration distributions. We did not average group medians. The reference mix stayed fixed for every omitted-year check.

The 71 records with unclear ages remain in the original whole-capture results. They are excluded from both comparisons above, so that exclusion is not mistaken for an adjustment effect.

Leaving out 2006 kept the adjusted median correlation positive (+0.831). After separately removing a linear calendar-year trend from temperature and median pace, their residual correlation was +0.912. This addresses only a straight-line trend.

The calculation reused saved inputs, the existing parser and fixed 09:00–15:00 Europe/Berlin ERA5 context. Independent export reading and weighted-quantile checks agreed. A separate calculation verified 204 correlation values and 3 year-sensitivity values. Offline replays reproduced the frozen analytical outputs.

The public checker reproduces counts, reference proportions and coefficients from aggregates. Reproducing weighted quantiles from source records requires the retained private inputs. The two readers share a research source; agreement does not authenticate that source.

How do we know the numbers hold up?

We checked 541,730 captured results, compared two calculation paths and reproduced the saved outputs. The analysis still has only 16 edition-level weather observations.

How we checked

This grouping study ran offline against saved inputs, checked against the original research file and prepared exports. The page serves the checked summaries. It does not recalculate them.

Median reported duration divided by 42.195 km. Temperature is the median of seven hourly ERA5 observations from 09:00 to 15:00 Europe/Berlin at the reviewed finish-area proxy. The window is independent of finishing durations. The primary fastest-20 split and fastest-50 sensitivity were fixed before these subgroup calculations.

Identical anonymous records remain separate. Independent export parsing checks counts and duration distributions. A separate covariance calculation verifies 136 group correlations and 51 whole/recorded-group correlations. Replays matched the retained analytical outputs.

These are anonymised research captures from the CC0-declared Kaggle version 2 dataset, not verified complete official fields. Timing basis and individual starts are unknown. Count gaps remain: 2008 has 79 more captured records than the published total; the 2013 published total is unknown. Course-era evidence does not prove identical routes.
